The Indian Keyboard Starter Guide

1. Form Factors & Layouts

Before buying, decide how much desk space you want to give up:

  • Full Size (100%): Includes numpad. Great for data entry, huge footprint.
  • Tenkeyless (80% / TKL): Drops the numpad. The sweet spot for general computing and space management.
  • 75% Layout: Squishes the arrow keys and function row together tightly. Highly recommended for custom starters.
  • 60% / 65% Layout: Hyper-minimalistic. 60% completely drops dedicated arrow keys. 65% retains them.

2. Anatomy of custom switch signatures

Switches break down into three primary mechanical engineering types:

  • Linear: Smooth travel from top to bottom without tactile disruptions (e.g., Red switches). Favored heavily by gamers.
  • Tactile: Provides a physically pronounced sensory bump right around actuation depth (e.g., Brown/Tactile variants). Ideal for typists who demand physical indexing.
  • Clicky: Features both a tactile bump and a distinct acoustic metal click return profile (e.g., Blue switches). Warning: Loud in quiet workspaces.

3. Beginner Modding Priorities

Want a better-sounding profile out of your entry board? Focus on these entry-level mods:

  • The Tape Mod (Tempest Mod): Apply 1-2 layers of painters/masking tape onto the back of the PCB. This filters out harsh high frequencies, giving your board a deep "thocky" output signature.
  • Stabilizer Tuning: Standard factory stabilizers rattle loudly. Clip your stem feet, apply a safe dielectric lubricant (like Permatex or Krytox 205g0) directly on the wire points to isolate friction.
  • Case Dampening: Pack the bottom of your empty plastic housing with acoustic foam or silicone pours to deaden empty interior sound reflections.
